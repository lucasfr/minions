## What YASA's spindle algorithm does (and the old mrpheus did not)

Read `yasa.spindles_detect`, `stft_power`, `moving_transform`, `_merge_close`, `_detrend_linear`, `trimbothstd`, MNE's auto transition bands.

- Two FIR filters (MNE, Hamming, zero phase) on the continuous signal: broadband 1-30 Hz (automatic transition bands: 1 Hz low, 7.5 Hz high) and sigma 12-15 Hz (1.5 Hz transitions).
- Criteria at every sample: relative sigma power of an STFT of the broadband signal (2 s windows, 0.2 s step, linear interpolation); moving correlation of sigma vs broadband (0.3 s window, 0.1 s step, linear interpolation); moving RMS of sigma with threshold mean + 1.5 trimmed SD (10% cut) over the mask, capped at 10 uV. Samples outside the stage mask set to 0, sum smoothed with a 0.1 s boxcar (`np.convolve(..., "same")`: offset w//2 before, (w-1)//2 after), keep where the smoothed count exceeds n_thresh - 1; merge gaps shorter than 500 ms; duration 0.5-2 s exclusive.
- Properties per event: detrended broadband peak-to-peak and RMS, sigma peak-to-peak, median STFT relative power over the frames inside the event, median log10 instantaneous power and median positive instantaneous frequency of the Hilbert analytic signal (zero padded to `scipy.fftpack.next_fast_len`, which equals the next 5-smooth number), number of peaks 60 ms apart, position and symmetry of the most prominent peak.
- Details that matter and are reproduced: scipy's own float arithmetic for STFT frame times and frequencies (`rfftfreq`: for 256 Hz the step is 52 samples, 0.203 s, not 0.2 s); `np.arange` length and values for the moving-transform grid; truncation with `astype(int)`; `np.interp` zero outside the range; scipy's `find_peaks` distance rule (peaks sorted by height) and prominence.
- Not implemented: `multi_only`, `remove_outliers`, coupling.
